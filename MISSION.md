# Mission: Engineering with AI (Claude Code / Codex)

## Why
After 30 years teaching physics, 10 as a gestalt psychotherapist, and 2 as an executive coach, you're returning to the UK at 66 and don't want to retire — you want to use this moment of AI capability to build something real. The pull is less "learn to code" and more "learn to engineer": go from idea to working, trustworthy prototype using Claude Code/Codex as your build partner.

## Success looks like
- You can take a vague idea (e.g. "a coaching support tool") and turn it into a working, runnable prototype within days, not months
- You can evaluate whether an AI-built tool *feels* right — trustworthy, grounded, not sycophantic — not just whether it technically works
- You understand enough of the underlying mechanics (git, repos, local vs. cloud, how Claude Code actually operates) to direct the work confidently, even without writing most code yourself
- You've shipped at least one small, real tool drawing on your coaching/therapy/teaching background — even if rough
- You have working governance habits on both fronts (see below), not just a finished feature

## Governance
This mission treats "governance" as two distinct disciplines, both deliberately learned rather than assumed:

- **Governance of the tool itself** — if anything built ever touches a real client/student relationship (coaching, therapy-adjacent, or teaching), it must have explicit safeguards: clear boundaries on what it should never claim to do (e.g. never substitute for a licensed therapist in crisis), confidentiality handling for any personal data, and honesty about being AI-generated rather than presenting as human judgment.
- **Governance of your own process** — you stay the decision-maker over what Claude Code/Codex builds. This means: reviewing diffs/changes before accepting them, understanding *why* a change was made (not just that it works), never letting "it runs" substitute for "I understand and approve of this," and asking follow-up questions whenever something is unclear rather than rubber-stamping.

Both forms of governance should show up explicitly in lessons — not as a one-off lecture, but as a running thread.

## Constraints
- Most evenings/weekends, not full-time
- Non-coder by background; learning through doing, not abstract theory first
- Comfortable in browser-based Claude Code; building confidence with terminal/VS Code locally
- Has a GPU Linux machine, interested in local LLMs as a side thread, not the main focus

## Out of scope (for now)
- Becoming a professional software engineer / deep CS theory
- Local LLM setup (separate interest, not blocking this mission)
- Committing to one specific product idea — this mission protects room to explore feel/UX/trust questions before locking a feature set
